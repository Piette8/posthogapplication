01 · Product understanding

PostHog is not just analytics. It's a Product OS for product engineers.

PostHog helps technical product teams understand usage, debug issues, test changes, roll out features, ask users questions, and keep product and customer data in one place. The buyer is not looking for a generic SaaS pitch. They want specific context, technical clarity, transparent pricing, and a real reason to spend time with sales.

01
Product engineers first
PostHog is built for people who want to try it, inspect docs, understand how things work, and solve product problems directly. That changes how outbound has to feel.
02
More than a point solution
Product analytics shows what happens. Session replay and error tracking help explain why. Feature flags and experiments help teams act. Surveys, CDP and data warehouse add more context.
03
Self-serve by default
Transparent usage-based pricing and generous free tiers mean sales has to earn its place. Not gatekeep access to the product.
04
When sales becomes useful
Sales adds value when the account is evaluating multiple products, scaling usage, migrating from fragmented tools, expanding across teams, or trying to connect PostHog to a real business objective.
🦔
If this message could sell any analytics tool, bin it.
02 · Outbound motion

The system already exists. The opportunity is to make the loop sharper.

From the public handbook: PostHog is not doing outbound to become a traditional enterprise-sales company. The goal is to build outbound early so it can scale intelligently before inbound slows down, without copying boring sales motions or damaging the brand.

01
Warm, warmish, cold
PostHog already thinks in levels of warmth: current users, engaged users, low/no-spend users, past signups, people who know PostHog, and people who don't yet.
02
The BDR lane
The BDR opportunity is mostly the warmish lane: accounts with enough context to justify outreach, but not enough momentum to become inbound by themselves.
03
The real goal
Not "get a meeting." Help the prospect decide whether PostHog solves a real problem. If it doesn't, disqualify or redirect. That's still a useful outcome.
04
The data foundation
Salesforce, ClickHouse, Customer.io, Clay, Lemlist, Harmonic, product usage signals and job-switcher signals are already part of the motion.
I'd start where signal is strongest: warmish accounts, job switchers, product-led intent. But I'd also test colder high-ICP accounts when public context justifies a useful, non-generic approach.

What I would not assume: that PostHog outbound is broken. What I would assume: there are opportunities to improve signal quality, account context, qualification notes, AE handoffs and feedback loops.
🦔
They already built pipes. Don't show up with a bucket.
03 · Account intelligence

Outbound should start with an account hypothesis, not a sequence.

PostHog's account planning framework asks the right questions: business model, product experience, hiring signals, stakeholders, current usage, underused products, risks and timing. I'd apply the same logic to outbound. Not a heavy account plan for every lead, just enough context to answer: why this account, why now, and why PostHog.

01
Business context
How does this company make money? What stage are they at? What metrics probably matter: activation, retention, conversion, expansion?
02
Product and hiring context
What does their product feel like? What roles are they hiring for: product, growth, data, experimentation, AI? What does that say about the next 12 months?
03
PostHog context
Are they already using PostHog? Which products? How mature is the usage? Is there an underused product that maps to a real business objective?
04
Stakeholder and timing context
Who owns the pain (product engineer, CTO, VP Product, founder)? Is there a launch, funding round, replatforming, or champion move that changes the timing?

05 · Plays

Three places I'd start

I wouldn't try to rebuild outbound. I'd start inside the existing motion, close to the work, and test where account understanding improves pipeline quality.

Warmish product-led accounts
Signal: meaningful product engagement, low or no spend, signs of underused product potential
Play 01

Working assumption: Some accounts may not need a sales call. They may need a better path to the next useful PostHog product, clearer context, or help connecting usage to a business objective.

Test
Does outreach work better when framed as product guidance rather than sales discovery?
Success
Product-relevant replies, better self-serve activation, trial momentum, AE-accepted opportunities with clear pain.
Stop if
Replies show no clear pain, no urgency, or that sales adds friction rather than value.
Job-switcher moments
Signal: someone who knows PostHog moves into a new company, team or role
Play 02

Working assumption: They may be in a moment where product and data tooling decisions are being made or revisited, but only if their new scope actually touches the problem.

Test
Can "you know PostHog" become a useful conversation about their new stack, without sounding opportunistic?
Success
Replies that clarify stack timing, current workflow, buyer path, and whether PostHog is already in consideration.
Stop if
They know PostHog but have no ownership, no timing, or no connection to the product and data workflow.
Creative multi-channel experiments
Signal: high-ICP account with a clear product or account trigger
Play 03

Working assumption: Normal email may not earn attention from a technical buyer who gets a lot of outreach. Something useful, specific, and slightly weird might.

Test
Email versus email plus LinkedIn plus Loom plus a small weird artifact. Does creative outreach get better replies from the right people?
Success
Meaningful replies that reveal pain, timing or buyer path. Not just positive responses.
Stop if
The weirdness becomes the point instead of the usefulness. Or if the account is clearly a bad fit regardless of format.

If the meeting is useless, the calendar lied.
06 · 90-day plan

90 days: earn the motion, then improve it

Not a fixed plan. It's how I'd think about earning trust: start close to the work, understand what PostHog already has, then systematize only what improves qualified pipeline.

1
DAYS 1–30
Learn what "good" looks like
Understand the product suite, buyer personas, pricing logic and why sales gets involved. Learn the current outbound segmentation, signal definitions, Salesforce and Clay workflows, AE expectations, and what a strong handoff looks like. Execute close to the work: accounts, messages, replies, qualification, DQ reasons.
Not over-automating before understanding the data. Not assuming my approach beats what already exists.
2
DAYS 31–60
Improve the account hypothesis layer
Test which signal and message combinations create useful replies, problem agreement and AE-accepted opportunities. Build lightweight account hypotheses before outreach. Improve handoff notes and DQ reasons so the team can see what is working and what is noise.
Not mistaking personalization for relevance. Not scaling plays based on volume alone.
3
DAYS 61–90
Systematize what deserves to scale
Turn the strongest patterns into reusable workflows: account brief templates, signal-to-hypothesis logic, qualification notes, Clay improvements and feedback loops. Kill weak signals and make it easier to repeat what works.
Only scaling once the data shows quality: useful replies, qualified pain, clean handoffs, low brand risk and AE acceptance.

Metrics I'd watch

Meaningful reply rate
Replies that reveal pain, context or timing. Not just positive responses.
Qualified conversation rate
Conversations where a real problem gets discussed and explored.
AE acceptance
Does the AE agree this account is worth pursuing after the handoff?
DQ quality
Are we disqualifying for clear reasons that improve future targeting?
🦔
Scale slowly. Spam quickly dies. Hedgehogs remember.
