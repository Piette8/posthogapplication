1 Executive Overview

2 Outbound Philosophy

What PostHog-style outbound is — and isn't

If a product engineer would roll their eyes, rewrite it or don't send it.

✓ PostHog outbound is

  • Specific — based on a real signal, not vague account fit
  • Technical — written for people who build, ship, and analyze products
  • Transparent — the prospect knows why they're being contacted
  • Permission-based — earn the right to pitch
  • Disqualification-friendly — if PostHog isn't relevant, say so
  • Brand-protective — increase respect even when prospects don't reply

✗ PostHog outbound is NOT

  • Generic SaaS outbound
  • "Checking in" sequences
  • Fake flattery about company growth
  • A procurement-first enterprise motion
  • Dragging small self-serve accounts into sales cycles
  • A pitch before understanding stack, pain, impact, timing

Rules of engagement

No campaign without a signal

Every touch ties to usage, job move, hiring, stack, launch, website intent, or funding.

Lead with a hypothesis

Say "usually this creates X" rather than pretending to know internal pain.

Ask permission before pitching

"Would it be useful if I showed how teams solve this in PostHog?"

Qualify before selling

Pain + impact + timing + buyer path determine the next step.

Respect self-serve

Low-potential accounts go to docs, not enterprise cycles.

Protect the brand

If a product engineer would roll their eyes, rewrite it or don't send it.

Language guide

✗ Never say

unlock insights
streamline workflows
empower teams
single pane of glass
quick sync
checking in / touching base
drive efficiency
revolutionize your analytics

✓ Say instead

"Looks like your product stack may be split across analytics, replay, and flags."
"If your team ships weekly but tests monthly, there may be a learning-speed gap."
"Funnels show the drop-off. Replay shows what confused the user."
"Internal feature flags are fine until maintaining them becomes its own roadmap."
"Worth checking if PostHog would remove some of this context switching?"

5 Persona Playbooks

Know who you're talking to — and what they care about

Each persona has different pain. Lead with theirs, not your pitch.

💻 Product Engineer

Cares about: Shipping, instrumentation, flags, experiments, debugging
Pain: Context switching across analytics / replay / flags
Best angle: One stack to understand, test, and ship
Avoid: Marketing analytics language
"When something underperforms, where do you go first: dashboard, replay, logs, or warehouse?"

📈 Growth Engineer

Cares about: Activation, attribution, funnels, experiments
Pain: Cannot connect source to activated/retained users
Best angle: Acquisition + product behavior in one loop
Avoid: Generic lead-gen language
"Can you connect channel source to activated users without stitching GA4, warehouse, and product analytics?"

📋 VP Product / Head of Product

Cares about: Roadmap quality, adoption, retention, product velocity
Pain: No trusted view across teams
Best angle: Shared operating layer for product decisions
Avoid: Tool-feature dumping
"Does your team trust one shared product view, or do different teams bring different numbers?"

🚀 Head of Growth

Cares about: PLG efficiency, activation, conversion, channels
Pain: Attribution split from product behavior
Best angle: Find growth drivers earlier
Avoid: Pure analytics dashboard pitch

🛠 CTO / VP Engineering

Cares about: Engineering focus, reliability, tooling debt, security
Pain: Internal tooling maintenance burden
Best angle: Reduce internal product-infra burden
Avoid: Cheap tool consolidation only
"Is any internal analytics/experimentation tooling becoming its own roadmap?"

📊 Data / Product Analyst

Cares about: Data trust, schema, SQL, usage analysis
Pain: Dashboard archaeology and data trust issues
Best angle: Trusted event data + AI-assisted analysis
Avoid: Undermining their role
"Which product questions still require SQL or analyst support before teams can act?"
6 Pain Point Messaging Library

Turn customer pains into outbound-ready messaging

Each pain is ranked by frequency and mapped to real PostHog customer proof points.

Pain #1

Fragmented product data — no trusted source of truth

"data was fragmented"; "we didn't have a full view"
Proof: Supabase 10x weekly new users • YC +30% data captured vs GA
Most teams don't lack data — they lack one trusted place to act from.
Pain #2

Slow experimentation velocity

"A/B testing had a lot of friction"; "1-2 experiments per month"
Proof: YC +40% messages, +35% successful matches
If you ship weekly but test monthly, learning speed lags shipping speed.
Pain #3

Shipping without seeing real behavior

"we observed drop-offs"; "without session replays"
Proof: Hasura +10-20% conversion improvement
Funnels show where users drop. Replay shows why.
Pain #4

Engineering time wasted on internal tooling

"maintenance burden became too much"
Proof: ResearchGate at 25M+ users
Internal tools work until maintaining them becomes the roadmap.
Pain #5

Weak attribution / growth driver visibility

"challenging to do basic attribution"
Proof: Supabase identified AI builders as acquisition source
Your next growth channel may already be in product data.

7 Campaign Library

Highest-priority outbound plays

Warmbound and job-switcher campaigns come first. Cold named-accounts are the second layer.

Priority 1 — Warm

Warmbound Expansion

Target:

Accounts already using PostHog with meaningful usage, product count, team growth, or billing signals.

Gate:

Must show plausible $20k+ ARR path.

Message:

"Your team is already using X. Teams at this stage often get value by adding Y because it connects Z."

CTA:

"Worth reviewing where your current PostHog setup could remove another tool or workflow?"

Priority 2 — Warmish

Job Switchers

Target:

Former PostHog users, champions, followers, or admins who moved into a new relevant role.

Trigger:

New job at a company with product-led motion or fragmented stack.

Message:

"You used PostHog before. Thought it might be relevant as you build the product stack at [new company]."

CTA:

"Worth sending over a short map of where PostHog usually fits at this stage?"

Priority 3 — Cold

PLG DevTools — Fragmented Stack

Target:

Series A-C devtool / infra / OSS-led SaaS with docs, free tier, self-serve, and stack sprawl.

Trigger:

Detected Amplitude/Mixpanel/Segment/GA4 plus flags/replay/warehouse tools; hiring product/growth engineers.

Message:

"Most devtool teams don't lack data; they lack one trusted place where usage, replay, flags and experiments connect."

Priority 4 — Cold

AI Product Teams Shipping LLM Features

Target:

AI SaaS / AI infra teams launching LLM workflows, evals, copilots, agents, or usage-based products.

Trigger:

AI feature launch, LLM analytics need, usage-based pricing, hiring product/growth/data roles.

Message:

"AI teams ship fast, but the harder question is which workflows users actually return to."

9 Discovery & Qualification

Structure calls around real pain — not a demo script

The goal isn't to close in one call. It's to decide whether PostHog solves a real problem, whether the account has commercial potential, and whether there's a concrete next step.

Discovery flow

1

Be transparent

Explain why you reached out and what signal triggered the conversation.

2

Understand role

What does this person own, and do they feel the pain directly?

3

Map current stack

Analytics, replay, flags, experiments, warehouse, attribution, error tracking.

4

Find specific pain

What's slow, trusted poorly, duplicated, expensive, or hard to debug?

5

Quantify impact

Time wasted, experiments delayed, conversion issue, tool cost, engineering opportunity cost.

6

Check timing

Is there a live project or a 6-month buying window?

7

Check authority

Buyer, champion, evaluator, or route to buyer?

8

Ask permission to pitch

Only after pain is clear.

9

Ask the hard question

"What would prevent this from becoming a priority?"

10

Set next step on the call

Trial, technical demo, team intro, async Loom, docs, or close out.

Qualification checklist

Specific pain

✓ They describe a concrete workflow/problem
✗ Only vague curiosity

Impact

✓ Time, money, growth, reliability, or focus cost
✗ No meaningful impact

Timeline

✓ Project or likely action within 6 months
✗ No timing or urgency

Authority / path

✓ They own the problem or can bring the buyer
✗ No buyer path

$20k+ ARR path

✓ Usage, team size, or expansion can justify sales
✗ Better handled via self-serve/docs

Product fit

✓ PostHog products map to the pain
✗ Problem outside PostHog scope

Disqualification-friendly responses

"Sounds like this isn't a live problem. I won't push for a meeting — here are the docs if useful."
"If the main need is just lightweight web analytics, self-serve is probably the better path for now."
"If there's no expansion path here, I'd rather not waste your time with a sales process."
"This sounds more like a data warehouse / BI problem than a PostHog problem."

Stop / continue / iterate rules

Stop if...

Replies show the pain hypothesis is wrong or too generic. Negative replies indicate brand damage.

Continue if...

Prospects repeat the pain in their own words. Problem agreement is consistent.

Iterate if...

Positive replies exist but CTA conversion is weak. Scale only when repeatable.

12 Implementation Roadmap

First 90 days — from setup to pipeline

Start with warmbound and job switchers. Cold named-accounts are the test, not the main motion.

Week 1

Foundation

Set data model, define scoring, build warmbound and job-switcher lists. Output: account table, signal taxonomy, scoring model, first sequences.

Week 2

First campaigns live

Launch warmbound/job-switcher campaigns and first small cold test. Output: reply analysis, qualification learnings, copy iteration.

Weeks 3–4

Scale winning signals

Add PLG devtool and AI product campaigns. Output: segment scorecard, persona performance, booked meetings.

Days 31–60

Operationalize

Salesforce routing, Clay enrichment, weekly outbound review cadence. Output: repeatable GTM workflow, dashboards, quality control.

Days 61–90

Expand & iterate

Additional signals, closed-lost reactivation, event lists, competitor consolidation plays. Output: second wave campaigns and pipeline model.

Risks & failure modes to avoid

Turning PostHog outbound into generic SDR cadences. Treating PLG devtools as the global priority instead of the best cold segment. Ignoring the $20k+ ARR path and creating low-value sales work.

Sending outreach without a clear signal. Overusing customer metrics without context. Selling too early before discovering pain, stack, timing, and buyer path.
