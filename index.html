1 Outbound Philosophy

If a product engineer would roll their eyes, don't send it

PostHog's buyers are technical. They read docs before taking demos. They'll Google you after your email. Outbound has to earn credibility, not demand attention.

✓ PostHog outbound is

  • Signal-based — tied to a real trigger, not vague account fit
  • Technical — written for people who build and ship products
  • Transparent — the prospect knows exactly why you're reaching out
  • Permission-based — earn the right to pitch, don't force a demo
  • Brand-protective — increase respect even when they don't reply
  • Disqualification-friendly — if PostHog isn't the answer, say so

✗ PostHog outbound is NOT

  • Generic SaaS sequences that could be about any product
  • "Just checking in" — the phrase that kills credibility
  • Fake flattery about their Series B or headcount growth
  • A procurement-first enterprise motion
  • Dragging self-serve accounts into sales cycles they don't need
  • Pitching before understanding stack, pain, and timing

Rules of engagement

No campaign without a signal

Every touch ties to usage, job move, hiring, stack change, or product launch.

Lead with a hypothesis

"Usually this creates X" — not pretending you know their internal pain.

Ask permission before pitching

"Would it be useful if I showed how teams solve this in PostHog?"

Qualify before selling

Pain + impact + timing + buyer path determine the next step. Period.

Respect self-serve

If they're better off in docs, send them to docs. That's a win.

Protect the brand

Would this email still make PostHog look good if they screenshot it on Twitter?

Language guide

✗ Never say

unlock insights
streamline workflows
empower teams
single pane of glass
quick sync
checking in / touching base
revolutionize your analytics

✓ Say instead

"Your product stack looks split across analytics, replay, and flags."
"If your team ships weekly but tests monthly, learning speed lags shipping speed."
"Funnels show the drop-off. Replay shows what confused the user."
"Internal feature flags are fine until maintaining them becomes its own roadmap."

3 Personas & Pain Messaging

Know who you're talking to — and lead with their pain, not your pitch

Each persona has different pain. The messaging library maps those pains to real PostHog customer proof points.

💻 Product Engineer

Cares about: Shipping, instrumentation, flags, experiments, debugging
Pain: Context switching across analytics / replay / flags
Best angle: One stack to understand, test, and ship
Avoid: Marketing analytics language
"When something underperforms, where do you go first: dashboard, replay, logs, or warehouse?"

📈 Growth Engineer

Cares about: Activation, attribution, funnels, experiments
Pain: Cannot connect source to activated/retained users
Best angle: Acquisition + product behavior in one loop
Avoid: Generic lead-gen language
"Can you connect channel source to activated users without stitching GA4, warehouse, and product analytics?"

🛠 CTO / VP Engineering

Cares about: Engineering focus, reliability, tooling debt, security
Pain: Internal tooling maintenance burden
Best angle: Reduce internal product-infra burden
Avoid: Cheap tool consolidation pitch
"Is any internal analytics/experimentation tooling becoming its own roadmap?"

📋 VP Product / Head of Product

Cares about: Roadmap quality, adoption, retention, product velocity
Pain: No trusted view across teams
Best angle: Shared operating layer for product decisions
Avoid: Tool-feature dumping
"Does your team trust one shared product view, or do different teams bring different numbers?"

Pain → proof point → outbound angle

Pain #1

Fragmented product data — no trusted source of truth

"data was fragmented"; "we didn't have a full view"
Proof: Supabase 10x weekly new users • YC +30% data captured vs GA
Most teams don't lack data — they lack one trusted place to act from.
Pain #2

Slow experimentation velocity

"A/B testing had a lot of friction"; "1-2 experiments per month"
Proof: YC +40% messages, +35% successful matches
Ship weekly but test monthly? Learning speed is behind shipping speed.
Pain #3

Shipping without seeing real behavior

"we observed drop-offs"; "without session replays"
Proof: Hasura +10-20% conversion improvement
Funnels show where users drop. Replay shows why.
Pain #4

Engineering time on internal tooling

"maintenance burden became too much"
Proof: ResearchGate at 25M+ users
Internal tools work until maintaining them becomes the roadmap.

R

Raycast

Developer productivity platform • Series B ($30M) • ~40 employees • Berlin HQ

ICP Score: 22/30

Signals detected

  • PLG devtool with extensions marketplace — strong self-serve motion
  • ~75% of team are engineers — engineering-led buying
  • Pro plan + Teams plan = monetization through product adoption
  • Extensions ecosystem = complex feature adoption tracking need
  • EU-based = relevant for PostHog's data residency story

Pain hypothesis

Raycast's value depends on which extensions users adopt and stick with. With a marketplace model, they need to know which extensions drive retention vs. which get installed and forgotten. That's a product analytics + experimentation problem, not a GA4 problem.

Target persona

Product Engineer working on the extensions platform or onboarding flow. They care about instrumentation, not dashboards. They want to know "which extensions correlate with 30-day retention?" without filing a ticket to the data team.

Why not a different persona?

At 40 people, there's probably no VP Product or Head of Growth yet. The product engineer who owns the extensions platform IS the buyer. Going above them would feel like going around them — that's a brand risk.

6 Discovery & Qualification

Structure calls around real pain — not a demo script

The goal isn't to close in one call. It's to decide if PostHog solves a real problem, whether there's commercial potential, and whether there's a concrete next step.

Discovery flow

1

Be transparent

Explain why you reached out and what signal triggered the conversation.

2

Understand role

What does this person own, and do they feel the pain directly?

3

Map current stack

Analytics, replay, flags, experiments, warehouse, attribution, error tracking.

4

Find specific pain

What's slow, trusted poorly, duplicated, expensive, or hard to debug?

5

Quantify impact

Time wasted, experiments delayed, conversion issue, tool cost, engineering opportunity cost.

6

Check timing & authority

Is there a live project? Are they the buyer, champion, or route to buyer?

7

Ask permission to pitch

Only after pain is clear. "Would it be useful if I showed how teams solve this?"

8

Ask the hard question

"What would prevent this from becoming a priority?"

9

Set next step on the call

Trial, technical demo, team intro, async Loom, docs, or close out.

Qualification checklist

Specific pain

✓ They describe a concrete workflow/problem
✗ Only vague curiosity

Impact

✓ Time, money, growth, reliability, or focus cost
✗ No meaningful impact

Timeline

✓ Project or likely action within 6 months
✗ No timing or urgency

Authority / path

✓ They own the problem or can bring the buyer
✗ No buyer path

Commercial potential

✓ Usage, team size, or expansion can justify sales
✗ Better handled via self-serve/docs

Product fit

✓ PostHog products map to the pain
✗ Problem outside PostHog scope

Disqualification-friendly responses

"Sounds like this isn't a live problem. I won't push for a meeting — here are the docs if useful."
"If the main need is lightweight web analytics, self-serve is probably the right path."
"If there's no expansion path, I'd rather not waste your time with a sales process."
"This sounds more like a data warehouse / BI problem than a PostHog problem."
7 90-Day Playbook

How I'd operationalize this — from day one to pipeline

The roadmap, the ops stack, and the metrics I'd track — all in one place. This is a hypothesis, not a prescription. I'd iterate weekly based on what the data says.

⚠ This is a starting plan, not a contract. I don't know PostHog's internal data yet. I don't know which signals are already being tracked or how product usage data flows to sales. The first week is about learning the real landscape and adjusting everything below.

Roadmap

Week 1

Learn the landscape

Understand PostHog's product usage data, CRM state, existing outbound learnings. Build first warmbound and job-switcher lists in Clay. Set up scoring model. Ship first sequences.

Week 2

First campaigns live

Launch warmbound expansion + job-switcher campaigns. Run one small cold test (PLG devtools). Analyze replies — are the pain hypotheses landing?

Weeks 3–4

Double down or kill

Scale what converts. Kill what doesn't. Add AI product team campaigns if cold test shows signal. Output: first qualified meetings, segment scorecard, copy iterations.

Days 31–60

Operationalize

Salesforce routing automated. Clay enrichment running weekly. Weekly outbound review cadence with the team. Start building repeatable playbooks by segment.

Days 61–90

Expand & compound

Add new signal sources: closed-lost reactivation, event lists, competitor consolidation plays. Build pipeline model. Contribute learnings back to PostHog's GTM playbook.

Metrics I'd track — connected to PostHog's outbound goals

Positive reply rate

Best early signal of message-market fit. Are the pain hypotheses resonating with technical buyers?

Problem-agreement rate

Do prospects confirm the pain in their own words? This matters more than meeting volume.

Qualified opportunity rate

The real north star. Are these turning into commercial conversations, not just demos?

Signal-level conversion

Which triggers justify outreach? Helps Clay and the scoring model get smarter over time.

Brand-risk indicators

Negative replies, unsubscribes, spam complaints. PostHog's brand is an asset — protect it.

DQ quality

A high disqualification rate means targeting is too broad. A low one means I'm not qualifying hard enough.

Stop / continue / iterate rules

Stop if...

Pain hypothesis is wrong. Negative replies indicate brand damage. No problem agreement after 50 sends.

Continue if...

Prospects repeat the pain in their own words. Problem agreement is consistent across the segment.

Iterate if...

Positive replies but weak CTA conversion. Scale only when the signal is repeatable in Clay.
